What this page promises, and what it refuses to do

Every other page of this platform answers its own question well. This one answers the question none of them could carry alone: is the machine turning, and which of its wheels are actually engaged? It owns no data and invents no calculation — it puts the cycles side by side without letting the comparison lie.

Three rules hold the whole page. The switch is shown beside the number: a count produced by a task nobody turned on measures the switch, not the market. The qualifier is in the same sentence as the number: the common failure here is not a wrong number, it is a right number that reads backwards — "26 rules registered" sounds like coverage when a research-stage rule cannot place an order and has its signals dropped from the vote. And a dash is not a zero: "—" means not measurable, 0 means measured at zero, and you do not act the same way on the two. Every count says what it counts over (now, 7 d, 30 d, all time), a code constant says constant, and a value turns amber only past its stated threshold, with ⚠ and its words.

The gap is live minus paper, in multiples of R. Negative is the normal direction: a simulated fill pays a modelled half-spread but never queues, never gaps through the stop, and is never partially executed. A heavily negative gap does not mean a rule that stopped working — it means a rule whose edge is smaller than its costs, and the two call for opposite fixes. A dash means one of the two venues closed nothing: that is not a small gap, it is the absence of measurement. Below {{ c.gap_floor }} closed trades on either venue (the desk's own evidence floor) a gap is printed and not judged: it is not coloured. "Live" here is the venue, paper=False over 180 days: the broker's demo fills count in it, as the desk counts them.
