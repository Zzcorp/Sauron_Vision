Lives as a partial for one reason: the signal_rail_partial view re-renders it when the dashboard WebSocket announces a new signal, so a fresh card can slide into the rail without a page reload. base.html includes it for the initial render; both consumers get identical markup by construction. s.acted (signals/models.py) is the join back to a live position — the manual path's exact metadata signal_id, or the bot path's rule_name string. A signal something has ALREADY acted on is marked here rather than removed: a card that vanishes is indistinguishable from one that expired or was dismissed, and the whole point is that the operator learns the engine acted. It ages out on its own lifecycle like any other card. Because the state is server-rendered it survives the WS partial swap for free — there is no client-side state to reapply. EVERY SIGNAL, AND WHY EACH ONE LEFT (2026-11-06). The rail lists every signal that can be acted on now (dashboard.signal_rail), newest first, not the five newest; it scrolls inside the rail. A signal leaves for a stated reason, and LEFT THE RAIL (hidden until its chip is pressed) keeps the last 24 hours of departures in words, with the TAKE TRADE ticket still offered while the engine holds the signal. Every row carries its facts as data-* attributes, which the rail's filters read (static/css/sv-rail.css); no row is ever edited by script. A signal whose market is shut now stays a card and says so (2026-11-12): the MARKET SHUT badge with the hour the market opens, data-market="shut" for the SHUT chip, and one line in its popup; an open market's card carries none of the three.
